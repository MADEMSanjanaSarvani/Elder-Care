Execution & Operations

Part 3 of the Project Setu PRD. The architecture in Part 2 is global-ready from day one — every table that would otherwise hardcode "India" or "₹" instead carries a region_id. This part keeps the actual build scoped to what Visakhapatnam needs, phase by phase, without pretending the roadmap is the architecture.

MVP scope: Visakhapatnam only Architecture scope: multi-city, multi-currency, multi-country from day one
17 — UI/UX Design Guidelines

Two products wearing one family of colors

The Family/Elder app and the Caregiver app need to feel like the same trustworthy institution while serving opposite jobs — one is reassurance and oversight, the other is a work queue. Same palette and type system, different density and pacing.

Paper #F2F4F0
Ink #15181A
Accent (trust/action) #B8691A
Verified/success #1D6C5F
SOS/critical #A83F2A

Elder mode — three buttons, not a menu

Home screen: Call for Help, Book Help, Today's Visit. Large text (min 18sp body, 24sp+ headers), voice input as a first-class alternative to typing everywhere text entry appears, high-contrast mode by default, and no UI element whose only affordance is a subtle color change — every state (verified, in-progress, urgent) gets a label, not just a hue.

Family mode — dashboard density, not clutter

Timeline-first home screen (today's visit, last AI summary, next reminder), booking and consent management one tap away. Consent controls are never buried in a settings submenu — they're a top-level tab, because hiding them undercuts the whole trust pitch.

Caregiver app — a work queue, not a brand showcase

Job list first, map second, everything else third. OTP entry and job-completion flows optimized for one-handed use standing in a doorway, and functional in 2G/patchy connectivity — this is the one surface where "looks polished" matters less than "works when the signal drops."

Language parity, not just translation

Copy is written natively per language for elder-facing screens (tone matters as much as accuracy — see Part 1's localization QA requirement), not machine-translated from an English master and never revisited.

18 — Flutter Project Structure

One shared core, two apps, no forked trust logic

Consent enforcement and trust-tier logic must never exist in two copies that can drift apart — that's how a consent bug ships in one app and not the other. Recommendation: a small monorepo (Melos-managed) with a shared package plus two thin app shells.

setu/ ├── packages/ │ └── setu_core/ # shared by both apps — the only place consent/trust logic lives │ ├── lib/ │ │ ├── region/ # region config client — reads GET /regions/:code/config once at startup │ │ ├── consent/ # consent-grant models + the has_consent() check mirrored client-side for UI gating │ │ ├── supabase_client/ # typed wrapper over the Supabase SDK + Edge Function calls │ │ ├── l10n/ # ARB files, one per launch language │ │ └── design_tokens/ # colors, type scale, spacing — Section 17 codified │ └── test/ ├── apps/ │ ├── family_elder_app/ │ │ └── lib/ │ │ ├── core/ # app-specific wiring: routing, DI, flavors (dev/staging/prod) │ │ └── features/ │ │ ├── auth/ data / domain / presentation, each │ │ ├── elder_profile/ │ │ ├── family_dashboard/ │ │ ├── booking/ │ │ ├── consent/ │ │ ├── sos/ │ │ ├── ai_assistant/ │ │ └── notifications/ │ └── caregiver_app/ │ └── lib/ │ ├── core/ │ └── features/ │ ├── auth/ │ ├── job_queue/ │ ├── otp_visit/ │ └── earnings/ └── melos.yaml

Each feature module follows data / domain / presentation (Clean Architecture): a repository talking to Supabase or an Edge Function, use-cases holding the actual business rules, and presentation widgets that know nothing about where the data came from. State management: Riverpod — its compile-time-safe dependency injection and native support for Supabase's realtime streams (SOS monitoring, live booking status) fit better here than a global store pattern, and it keeps use-cases testable without a running app.

19 — Development Roadmap

Six phases, one architecture

01
MVP · Visakhapatnam only

Prove the trust loop works

Everything in Part 1's MVP feature list, built against the Part 2 schema (which already carries region_id, currency, and compliance_profile — but only one row in regions is active). Success criteria: repeatable caregiver onboarding, a completed booking-to-payout cycle, a tested SOS path, and enough real bookings to validate willingness-to-pay for both the commission and subscription lines.

02
Andhra Pradesh & Telangana

Prove the playbook repeats

Adding a city is a new regions row plus local caregiver-network build-out and localized service pricing — not new code. This phase is where that claim gets tested for real; any place engineering has to special-case a city is a bug to fix before Phase 3, not a one-off.

03
Tier-1 India · Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Pune, Kolkata, Ahmedabad

Prove it scales operationally, not just technically

Volume growth stresses the caregiver-matching query and the verification queue's throughput, not the schema. This is the phase to revisit matching performance (Section 22) and to add city-level ops staffing, not to redesign the data model.

04
Nationwide India

Institutional partnerships come online

Hospitals, pharmacies, diagnostic labs, insurers, NGOs, and senior-care organizations plug in as B2B2C channels. This is also the natural point to formalize the DPO role and DPIA program flagged in Part 2, since institutional partners will ask for it directly.

05
NRI families

The payer moves abroad, the elder doesn't

International cards and multi-currency family billing via Razorpay's existing international support — no new payment adapter needed, because the elder is still India-resident and the India regions row still governs their record. This phase is mostly a payments and UX change (currency display, international phone/OTP handling for family accounts), not an infrastructure one.

06
International · US, UK, Canada, Australia, Singapore, UAE, Europe

The elder is resident abroad — this is the real expansion

New regions rows with a different country_code, compliance_profile (HIPAA, GDPR, etc.), payment_provider adapter (Stripe or local equivalent), and BGV vendor — and, per Section 15, a genuinely separate regional deployment where data residency law requires it. This is the phase the Part 2 architecture was built to make possible without a rewrite; it's still real, non-trivial work — new compliance review, new local caregiver-vetting partners, new healthcare-integration research per country — not a copy-paste of the India rollout.

21 — Deployment Plan

Boring, reversible, and observable

  • Environments: dev → staging → production Supabase projects, with per-PR Supabase preview branches for schema changes so a migration is reviewed against real branch data before it touches production.
  • Migrations: Supabase CLI migrations checked into the repo, applied through CI — never a manual change against the production database.
  • Edge Functions: versioned deploys with an immediate rollback path; the payment and SOS functions specifically get a canary step before full rollout.
  • Flutter release: Android via the Play Store's internal testing track first (matching the caregiver pool and pilot family cohort), graduating to production after the field UAT in Section 20 passes.
  • Feature flags per region: since regions.status already models planned/active/paused, Phase 2+ city launches are a flag flip plus content, not a new release train.
  • Secrets: managed via Supabase Vault / CI secret store; rotated on a schedule, never committed, never present in a Flutter build artifact.
22 — Scaling Strategy

Scale the parts that will actually be under load — not everything, preemptively

Caregiver matching

A straightforward SQL filter (trust tier, location radius, language, availability) is correct through Phase 2. Revisit only if Phase 3's Tier-1-city volume shows real query latency — don't build a dedicated matching service for a problem Visakhapatnam doesn't have yet.

Read-heavy admin queries

Analytics and reporting queries move to a read replica once the admin dashboard's query volume starts competing with booking-path writes for primary database resources — a Supabase-supported upgrade path, not a re-architecture.

Multi-currency & multi-country

Already modeled: regions.currency, tax_profile, and the PaymentProvider port from Part 2. Phase 6 adds adapters and rows, not new tables.

Translation content at scale

Ten languages by hand is feasible; beyond that (Phase 6's country-specific languages), move from ad hoc ARB file management to a translation-management platform (e.g. Lokalise/Crowdin) so native-speaker review stays sustainable.

Data residency & regional sharding

One Supabase project through Phase 5 (Section 15). Phase 6 introduces per-region deployments where law requires it — the regions table is the seam that split happens along, so it's additive, not a migration of existing India data.

AI cost & latency

Visit-summary and translation calls are the highest-volume AI usage; watch OpenAI cost-per-booking as volume grows and consider a cheaper model tier for translation specifically once usage justifies the engineering time to split it out.

23 — Risk Register

Carried forward from Part 1, plus what MVP execution adds

Marketplace chicken-and-egg in a single city

Not enough caregivers → slow bookings → families churn before caregiver supply catches up. Mitigation: hand-recruit and subsidize the first caregiver cohort in Visakhapatnam rather than waiting for organic supply; Phase 1 success criteria should include a minimum caregiver-to-household ratio, not just booking count.

High likelihoodPhase 1

Police-verification turnaround throttles caregiver activation

State processing time is outside the platform's control and can stall the standard trust tier for weeks. Mitigation: the probationary tier (IDfy-cleared, supervised jobs only) exists specifically so this doesn't block Phase 1 launch — but it needs enough probationary-tier job types to keep a new caregiver earning while they wait.

High likelihoodPhase 1

Payment settlement model challenged by RBI or Razorpay onboarding

The merchant-of-record decision (Part 1) still needs sign-off from payments/legal counsel and from Razorpay's own onboarding review before Phase 1 payments go live. Mitigation: start that conversation now, in parallel with engineering, not after the app is built.

Medium likelihood, high impactPhase 1

Consent UX friction reduces adoption

An elder-granted, per-category consent model (the right call for trust and DPDP alignment) is inherently more setup steps than "child manages everything." Mitigation: invest specifically in making the elder-side consent flow fast and voice-guided, since this is the one piece of extra friction the whole model depends on being worth it.

Medium likelihoodPhase 1

Vendor concentration — Supabase, Razorpay, OpenAI, IDfy

Four single-vendor dependencies at MVP. Acceptable for Phase 1-2 velocity; the PaymentProvider port and region-scoped bgv_provider field (Part 2) are the deliberate seams for reducing this risk later without a rewrite — but Supabase and OpenAI don't have an abstraction yet, which is a reasonable MVP trade-off, not an oversight, given switching cost vs. one-city-scale risk.

Low likelihood, high impactOngoing

International compliance risk arrives all at once in Phase 6

HIPAA, GDPR, and country-specific healthcare-integration requirements are each substantial independently. Mitigation: treat Phase 6 as a per-country compliance research project starting well before engineering work begins on that region, not a checklist to clear quickly.

Certain, low urgencyPhase 6
24 — Future Roadmap

Beyond the six phases

A few things worth deciding early even though they're not urgent: a real brand name (this document has been using "Project Setu" as a placeholder throughout — worth resolving before Phase 1 marketing needs it), when to bring on a dedicated clinical safety officer (recommend: no later than Phase 2, once clinical-tier bookings have real volume), and when to formalize the DPO role (recommend: ahead of Phase 4's institutional partnerships, since partners will ask). Future revenue lines flagged in Part 1 — pharmacy, diagnostics, telemedicine, equipment rental, caregiver certification — slot naturally into the Phase 4 partnership motion rather than needing their own roadmap track.
