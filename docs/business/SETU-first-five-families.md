# Finding your first five families

*Messages, scripts and what to watch for. Written for Vizag, ₹0 budget.*

Five is the number on purpose. One family teaches you nothing — you can't
tell a quirk from a pattern. Fifty is unmanageable when you're one person
and things break. Five is enough to see a pattern and few enough that you
can drive to each of them.

---

## Who you're actually looking for

Not "anyone with elderly parents". You want a specific person:

> **An adult son or daughter, 28–45, working in Bangalore / Hyderabad /
> Chennai / abroad, whose parents live alone in Vizag.**

They are the buyer, the installer, and the one who feels the pain. Their
parent is the user. Those are two different people and you must recruit
**both** — a daughter who installs it on her own phone but never gets her
mother to open it has taught you nothing.

**Prefer families where:**
- the parent already uses WhatsApp (they can learn SETU)
- there's a real worry — a recent fall, a missed medicine, a hospital visit
- the child calls home most days (they already have the habit you're replacing)

**Avoid, for now:**
- parents with advanced dementia — you're not ready for that duty of care
- families where a full-time attendant already lives in — no pain to solve
- anyone who'd sue you. Seriously. You have no insurance yet.

---

## Where to find them, in order of what actually works

1. **Your own circle first.** Your parents' friends. Your relatives. Your
   friends' parents. You need trust more than you need scale, and you have
   trust here. Expect 3 of your 5 from this list.
2. **Your parents as introducers.** "Aunty, my daughter has made an app for
   families like ours" carries more weight than anything you can say.
3. **Apartment-society WhatsApp groups** in Vizag — MVP Colony, Seethammadhara,
   Madhurawada. Ask the secretary before posting; posting cold reads as spam.
4. **Morning walkers** at Beach Road / Kailasagiri, 6–7 am. Talk to the
   elders directly, then ask for their child's number.
5. **A friendly local GP or diagnostic centre.** Not for referrals yet — just
   ask them what families struggle with. You'll learn more in one
   conversation than a week of guessing.

Do **not** start with paid ads, Instagram, or a launch post. You have
nothing to send traffic to yet.

## The messages

Send these on WhatsApp, one to one. Never in a group, never as a broadcast
list — people can tell, and it kills the trust you're trading on.

### To someone you know

> Hi [Name]! I've built something I'd really value your opinion on.
>
> It's called SETU — an app for families like ours where parents live in
> Vizag and the children are in another city. It shows you how their day
> went: did they take their medicines, did they check in, and there's an SOS
> button if something goes wrong.
>
> I'm looking for 5 families to try it free for a month and tell me honestly
> what's wrong with it. No cost, nothing to sign, and you can stop whenever.
>
> Would you and Uncle/Aunty be up for it? I'd come over and set it up myself.

### To someone a friend introduced you to

> Hi [Name], [Friend]'s daughter here — she suggested I reach out.
>
> I've made an app called SETU for families where parents are in Vizag and
> the children are working elsewhere. It gives you a picture of their day —
> medicines, a daily check-in, an SOS button — so you're not calling four
> times to check.
>
> I'm testing it with 5 families before launching. Free, for a month, and
> I'll set it up in person. Would you be open to a 10-minute call?

### What not to say

- ❌ "AI-powered elder care platform" — means nothing to a worried daughter
- ❌ "Revolutionary" / "first of its kind" — you don't know that
- ❌ "Emergency response" — you are not an ambulance service. Never imply it.
- ❌ Any promise about response times. You have no operations team yet.

---

## The first conversation (15 minutes, in person if you can)

**Spend the first 10 minutes listening, not demoing.** Ask:

1. "How often do you call them?"
2. "What do you actually worry about?"
3. "What happened the last time something went wrong?"
4. "Who checks on them if you can't reach them?"
5. "Has anyone in the family ever missed a medicine dose?"

Write down their exact words. Those words become your app copy and your
Play Store description — better than anything you or I would write.

**Then, five minutes:** show the dashboard on your phone. Not every screen.
Just: *this is their day, this is the medicine tracker, this is SOS.*

**Then ask directly:** "Can I set this up on your phone and Aunty's phone
right now?" A yes-in-principle that never becomes an install is a no.

---

## Be straight about what it is

Say this out loud, every time. It protects them and it protects you:

> "This is new and it will have bugs. SETU is **not** an emergency service —
> if something serious happens, call 112 or the hospital first. The SOS
> button alerts you and me, not an ambulance. I'm asking you to try it so I
> can find out what's broken."

Under-promising is not weakness here. These are people's parents. A family
that trusts you after a bug is worth more than one impressed by a demo.

---

## Setting it up (do this yourself, in person)

Budget 30 minutes per family:

1. Install the APK on **the child's** phone → they create the account
2. Install on **the parent's** phone → sign in, pick "Senior"
3. Add 2–3 real medicines with real timings
4. Do one check-in together so the parent has done it once
5. **Test SOS with them** so nobody presses it in a panic never having tried
6. Save your number in the parent's phone as "SETU — Sanjana"

Then leave. Don't hover. You want to see what happens without you.

---

## Week one: what to ask, and when

**Day 2** — one message to the child: *"Did Aunty open it yesterday?"*
**Day 4** — call the parent, not the child: *"Was anything confusing?"*
**Day 7** — 15-minute call with the child. Ask exactly these:

1. "Did you open it? How many times?"
2. "What did you check first?"
3. "Did it change whether you called them?"
4. "What annoyed you?"
5. "If I switched it off tomorrow, would you mind?"

Question 5 is the real one. A polite "it's nice" means nothing. *"Please
don't switch it off"* means you have something.

---

## The one number that matters

Not downloads. Not signups. This:

> **How many of the five parents opened the app on day 7 without being
> reminded?**

- **4–5** → you have something real. Get five more families.
- **2–3** → something works but the habit doesn't. Find out what the two who
  stuck with it have in common.
- **0–1** → the parent-side experience is wrong. Don't add features. Sit
  next to one parent and watch them use it for an hour.

Track it in a plain sheet — family, parent's name, day 1/3/7 opened, one
quote. Five rows. You don't need a CRM.

---

## Money — not yet

Don't charge these five. You're buying information, not revenue, and a free
pilot gets you honesty a paying customer won't give you.

But **do** ask at day 30: *"If this cost ₹499 a month, would you pay it?"*
Watch their face before you hear the answer. Then ask the better question:
*"What would it need to do for ₹499 to feel obvious?"*

---

## When to change direction

Be honest with yourself at day 30:

- **Parents aren't opening it** → the elder experience is the whole problem.
  Fix that before anything else.
- **Children look once and stop** → you're not delivering peace of mind,
  just data. The daily summary has to be worth reading.
- **Everyone asks for the same missing thing** → build exactly that, nothing
  else.
- **Nobody's life changed** → the honest answer might be that families want a
  *person*, not an app. That's a real finding, and it points at the
  caregiver-marketplace side of SETU instead. Better to learn it at five
  families than at five hundred.

---

## Your next seven days

- **Day 1** — list 15 names. Just names, on paper.
- **Day 2** — message the 5 warmest.
- **Day 3–4** — have the conversations. Listen more than you talk.
- **Day 5–6** — install with whoever says yes.
- **Day 7** — first check-ins.

Five families, one week. That's the whole task. Everything about the Play
Store can wait — a listing for an app nobody has used is a listing nobody
will find.
