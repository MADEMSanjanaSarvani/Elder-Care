# SETU — Founder's Handbook

*Every fundamental business question, Day 1 → billion-dollar company.*
*Written to be read by the founder, and to survive an investor, a hospital, a caregiver, and a regulator asking hard questions.*

> Companion to the feasibility plan, launch playbook and functional audit. This one is
> **pure business** — no code, no UI, no APIs. It is opinionated on purpose; a founder needs
> conviction, not a menu.

## Section 1 — Trust (the entire business)

**SETU is not selling an app. It is selling the belief that a stranger will treat your mother with dignity, and that help arrives when she falls.** Everything below serves that.

- **Why will users trust SETU?** Because trust is *engineered and visible*: every caregiver is background-verified + police-checked, carries a SETU ID, is rated by real families, and is insured. Families see who is coming, track the visit, and can raise an SOS that dials 108 first. Trust isn't claimed — it's shown on every screen.
- **Why choose SETU over competitors?** Most alternatives are either **informal** (a maid/attendant with no verification, no accountability) or **fragmented** (separate apps for doctor, medicine, nurse). SETU is the **only verified, coordinated, one-place elder-care system** with an emergency backbone. The wedge: *"With SETU, you always know your parent is safe — even from another city or country."*
- **How to build trust in month 1?** Over-serve the first 100 families personally. Founder-led onboarding calls. A dedicated care manager per family. Same caregiver every visit (continuity). Respond to every issue in minutes. Publish a public **Trust Page** (verification standards, insurance, SOS SLA). Trust in month 1 is manual and human — automate it only after it's proven.
- **Certifications to have:** business registration + GST, professional indemnity + public liability insurance, DPDP-compliant privacy program, caregiver skill certification via HSSC/NSDC or a nursing partner, and (as you grow) an ISO-27001-style data posture. Display them.
- **Uniforms + ID cards?** **Yes.** A visible SETU ID card (with photo, verified badge, QR to verify authenticity) and a light branded uniform/apron. It reassures the elder, signals professionalism, and deters impersonation. Low cost, huge trust ROI.
- **Police verification?** **Yes — mandatory.** Via a background-verification agency + a police character certificate for anyone doing live-in/overnight care. Re-verify annually.
- **How to prove caregivers are trustworthy?** Layered proof: verified badge, visible ratings + reviews, tenure, training badges, live-visit check-in (OTP + tracking), and insurance backing. The family sees the evidence, not a promise.
- **Training?** **Yes — mandatory onboarding** (dignity, consent, hygiene, SOS protocol, boundaries, data privacy) before the first job, plus skill top-ups (dementia, fall-prevention, medication) that unlock higher-paying work.
- **Ratings visible to users?** **Yes** — aggregate rating + reviews to families; anonymised feedback to the caregiver. Recent visits weighted higher.
- **How to ensure elder safety?** Verification + training + live tracking + 108-first SOS + medication reminders + daily check-ins + insurance + a fast incident-response runbook + spot audits.
- **How to gain public trust?** Transparency (publish standards + incident handling), testimonials, hospital/doctor endorsements, press on real outcomes, and — most powerfully — **never mishandling a safety incident.** One viral failure handled badly can end the company; one handled with integrity builds a decade of trust.

## Section 2 — Caregiver recruitment (the supply engine)

**End-to-end model:**

1. **Sourcing (layered):**
   - **Agencies (Day-1 liquidity):** partner with 3–4 home-healthcare agencies for pre-trained GDAs/nurses. Revenue-share, instant supply.
   - **Direct pool (the moat):** recruit from HSSC/NSDC GDA training institutes, ANM/GNM nursing colleges, retired nurses, and **caregiver referrals** (they know each other; pay a referral bonus).
   - **Adjacent talent:** **retired nurses** (premium "senior caregiver" tier — huge trust), **homemakers** (companionship + light care after training), **college students** (companionship/"digital help" visits only — never clinical or overnight).
2. **Screening:** application → ID/KYC (DigiLocker) → skill-cert check → 2 reference calls → **background + police verification** → structured video interview (empathy + scenario handling) → skills demo for clinical tasks.
3. **Approval:** verification lead decides; every decision logged. SLA 3–5 days.
4. **Training:** mandatory onboarding + skill tiers (certified).
5. **Activation:** ID card + uniform + app onboarding.
6. **Growth:** reliability score → dispatch priority; tiers (Bronze→Elite) unlock better pay/jobs; annual re-verification.
7. **Off-boarding:** clean contractor exit; instant suspension + investigation on any safety complaint.

**Direct-hire vs agency vs contractor?** Start with **independent contractors + agency supply.** Do **not** salary-employ at launch (PF/ESI/gratuity + idle-cost + misclassification risk). Build a small **salaried "Elite" cadre** only in Phase 3 for premium managed-care.

**Pay:** benchmark to local market — GDA visit ₹300–600/hr or ₹800–1,500/day (12h); live-in ₹1,200–2,000/day (metro). SETU takes ~20% (15% intro). Weekly payout + optional instant-payout perk. **Pay fairly and on time — supply trust is as fragile as customer trust.**

**Medical certifications?** Required for *clinical* tasks (nursing registration / GDA cert). Not required for pure *companionship* — but everyone completes SETU's safety onboarding + a health-fitness certificate.

## Section 3 — Elderly users

- **Why would they use SETU?** They mostly won't "use" it much — **the family is the primary user; the elder is the beneficiary.** For the elder, value = a trusted helper, medicine reminders, companionship, and one-button help. Design for *low interaction, high reassurance.*
- **Comfortable with technology?** Often not. So: voice-first, large text, three-action home, one-tap SOS, and — crucially — **the family operates most of it remotely.** The elder's burden is near-zero.
- **Simple for them?** Elder mode: big buttons, minimal choices, vernacular voice, no jargon. The best elder UX is *almost no UX.*
- **Don't know English?** Vernacular-first (Hindi/Telugu/Tamil…). Voice in their language. This isn't a feature — **it's the market** in India.
- **Live alone?** This is the **core ICP.** Daily check-ins, scheduled caregiver visits, SOS, and family alerts exist precisely for the elder living alone.
- **Dementia?** Guardian/legal-consent flow (family assumes control), wandering safeguards (opt-in), simplified UI, dementia-trained caregivers, routine reminders. A higher-ARPU, higher-trust tier.
- **No smartphone?** Then the **family's phone is the control centre**, and the elder is served via **caregiver visits + a simple call-based check-in / a low-cost SETU device later.** Never assume the elder has a smartphone — assume the *family* does.

## Section 4 — Families (the payer)

- **Why trust us?** Verification, insurance, transparency, live visibility, and human care managers. We make the invisible (what's happening at home) visible.
- **Why pay us?** Because the alternative is **guilt, anxiety, and unreliable informal help.** We sell *peace of mind and time* — the two things a distant adult child lacks most.
- **Emotional problem solved:** the guilt of not being there. The fear of "what if she falls and no one knows." SETU converts helpless worry into confident oversight.
- **Biggest fears:** (1) a bad/unsafe caregiver, (2) a missed emergency, (3) being deceived/overcharged, (4) their parent feeling abandoned. Every fear maps to a SETU feature (verification, SOS, transparent pricing/invoices, companionship + daily updates).
- **How to reassure:** daily timeline + photos + "your mother had a peaceful day" summaries, instant alerts, a human they can call, and visible proof at every step.

**The NRI wedge:** the highest-paying, highest-pain segment — market to them first.

## Section 5 — Business

- **How SETU makes money:** (1) subscriptions (recurring), (2) ~20% commission on services, (3) teleconsult margin, (4) premium/managed-care, (5) B2B2C (corporate + insurer). Subscriptions cover fixed cost; commission funds growth; managed-care + B2B2C expand ARPU.
- **Biggest expenses:** **not servers** — it's **trust-and-safety ops, caregiver verification/BGV, insurance, care-manager headcount, and city-launch subsidies.** Model these first.
- **Year-1 capital:** enough for one city's supply build + T&S ops team + insurance + 12–18 months runway + modest marketing. (Model it bottoms-up per city; the number is driven by ops headcount, not tech.)
- **MVP / build first:** verified caregiver booking + medication management + **SOS (108-first)** + daily check-ins + family visibility + a subscription. That's the trust-and-safety core.
- **Postpone:** in-app maps SDK, complex AI features, smart-home devices, multi-city, international — all Phase 2+. Ship the trust core, prove the loop, then expand.

## Section 6 — Operations

- **During emergencies:** 108/112 dialed **first** (mandatory), family + emergency contacts notified in parallel, nearest caregiver dispatched, SETU on-call ops alerted, incident report generated. SETU **coordinates**, it does not replace emergency services.
- **Who answers emergency calls?** An **on-call ops team** (small at first — even founder-led on rotation), escalating to a 24×7 care-desk as volume grows. Partner ambulance aggregator as secondary.
- **24×7?** Safety paths (SOS, alerts) must be **always-on** from Day 1 (even if it's a founder's phone at launch). General support can be business-hours early, moving to 24×7 with scale.
- **Employees needed (launch city):** a lean core — founders + a **verification/T&S lead**, a **caregiver-ops/supply manager**, **care managers** (1 per N families), **on-call ops**, and support. Tech is small; **ops is the org.**
- **Managing cities:** a repeatable **city-launch playbook** (supply targets, ops runbooks, SLAs) run by a city ops lead. Don't open a city until the previous one is a repeatable, break-even playbook.
- **How to scale:** density first (win one city deep) → replicate playbook city-by-city → ARPU expansion (managed care, B2B2C) → then geography. Scale **trust operations**, not just software.

## Section 7 — Legal

- **Who's responsible if something goes wrong?** SETU is positioned as an **intermediary/coordination platform**; care is delivered by independent verified caregivers / licensed RMPs who carry their own professional liability. But SETU carries **reputational + platform liability** — so **insurance (professional indemnity, public liability, caregiver accident, cyber) is non-negotiable**, and a clear incident-response + escalation process is mandatory.
- **Legal protections needed:** clean contractor + doctor + hospital agreements, lawyer-reviewed T&Cs with liability limits + SOS disclaimer, DPDP-compliant privacy program, insurance stack, entity/IP hygiene.
- **Policies users accept:** T&Cs, Privacy Policy, SOS/medical/AI disclaimers, cancellation + refund policy, consent management.
- **Disputes:** in-app dispute flow → auto-refund/credit for clear cases → ops mediation → T&S escalation for safety. Document everything.
- **Protect medical info:** consent-gated access, encryption, least-privilege, audit logging, DPDP compliance, breach runbook. Health data is the most sensitive asset — treat it as such.

## Section 8 — What each stakeholder will ask

- **Investors:** market size + why now, unit economics (CAC/LTV/contribution margin), supply liquidity, retention, moat, trust/safety track record, regulatory risk, team, use of funds. *Prove a repeatable, break-even city playbook.*
- **Hospitals:** who are your caregivers, how are they verified, liability boundaries, data handling, referral economics, response reliability.
- **Caregivers:** how much + how often do I get paid, how many jobs, what's the commission, is it safe, is it respectful, will you support me in a dispute.
- **Users/families:** is the caregiver safe/verified, what happens in an emergency, what does it cost (any hidden fees), can I cancel/refund, will the same person come.
- **Google Play:** data-safety disclosures, background-location justification, subscription/billing compliance, medical claims, account deletion, disclaimers.
- **Regulators:** DPDP compliance + grievance officer, telemedicine (NMC + Guidelines 2020), payment-aggregator compliance, labour/gig-welfare, emergency-service positioning, insurance.

## Section 9 — Worst-case scenarios + solutions

- **No caregivers (cold supply):** agency partnerships for Day-1 liquidity; referral bonuses; density-first geography; surge incentives; never open bookings below a minimum verified pool.
- **No one buys subscriptions:** validate willingness-to-pay with the NRI segment first; lead with the pain (safety/guilt), not features; offer a free safety tier as the on-ramp; iterate pricing; if it still won't convert, pivot to commission-led or B2B2C.
- **SOS fails:** 108/112 dialed directly (works offline via dial + SMS), redundant channels, escalation ladder, on-call ops, monitoring/alerting, drills. **Never advertise a guaranteed response time you can't uphold.**
- **Caregiver behaves inappropriately:** instant suspension, safeguarding runbook, police report if warranted, family support + insurance remediation, permanent ban, transparent handling. Prevent via vetting + training + ratings + audits.
- **Poor ratings:** treat as signal — retrain or remove; reliability score drives dispatch; protect quality relentlessly (a marketplace dies on quality, not price).
- **Legal complaints:** disclaimers + insurance + documented process + fast, empathetic resolution; legal counsel on retainer.
- **Emergency-service misuse / false alarms:** confirm/rate-limit triggers, practice/drill mode, repeat-offender tracking, education.
- **Payment disputes:** idempotent payments, reconciliation, clear invoices + refund policy, fast support, PA-backed dispute handling.

**Meta-solution:** over-invest early in **trust-and-safety ops + insurance.** The failure modes that kill this company are safety and trust failures, not tech failures.

## The one thing to remember
**SETU is a trust company.** Product, price, and tech are table stakes; the company that earns and never betrays a family's trust — across the entire arc of a parent's ageing — wins the market. Build the trust engine first, protect it obsessively, and everything else compounds on top.
