# SETU — Zero-to-Launch Handbook

*For a student founder, in Visakhapatnam, with ₹0, building it alone, launching tomorrow.*
*No funding assumed. No partnerships assumed. No employees assumed. Just you, a phone, and a real problem.*

> **Read this first.** You already have something 99% of idea-stage founders don't: **a working
> app.** But the app is NOT your advantage yet — **proof that families will pay is.** This
> handbook is about getting that proof for ₹0, then growing only as fast as the proof lets you.
> The single most important idea in this whole document: **validate manually before you spend
> anything.** You can run SETU with a WhatsApp group and your own phone before the app matters.

---

## The mindset (the antidote to the fear)
- **The 30-day goal is a *question*, not a *result*:** "Will 5 families in Vizag pay for verified elder care?" You're a scientist running an experiment, not a gambler betting your life.
- **Failure at ₹0 is not failure — it's a cheap, fast lesson.** The only real failure is spending money/years *before* you have proof.
- **You are not behind. You are early, and early + cheap + fast is the best position a founder can be in.**

## Section 1 — The ₹0 launch strategy

**What you build for free:** nothing new — you use what exists.
- **The "app" for your first 5 families can be YOU + WhatsApp + phone calls.** This is called a *concierge MVP*: you manually do what the app will eventually automate. This is how Airbnb, DoorDash, and most great startups started.
- **Free tools:** WhatsApp (coordination), Google Forms (caregiver applications + family sign-ups), Google Sheets (your "database" — bookings, caregivers, visits), Google Calendar (scheduling), a free Canva account (ID cards, flyers, logo), Gmail (comms), Google Maps (directions), a free Notion/Google Doc (your SOPs). **Your existing Supabase + app run on free tiers** when you're ready.
- **Free APIs / services:** Supabase free tier, Firebase free tier (push), Razorpay (no monthly fee — pay-per-transaction only), GitHub (free). You already have these.
- **What to NOT pay for yet:** paid ads, an office, salaried staff, a company registration (you can operate as a sole proprietor initially), maps SDK, SMS/DLT, custom devices, a designer, a lawyer's full retainer (use templates now, review later).
- **MVP features required:** verified caregiver visit + medication reminders + a way to reach help (SOS = a phone number you answer) + daily update to the family. That's it.
- **Postpone:** AI companion, video consults, subscriptions/billing automation, in-app maps, multi-language, devices, admin dashboards.

**The ₹0 loop:** find 1 caregiver you trust → find 1 family that needs help → manually match them → deliver a great week → collect ₹ (cash/UPI) → get feedback → repeat. **No code required to start.**

## Section 2 — MVP: the 5 features (and the order)

**Launch with these 5, in this priority:**
1. **Verified caregiver visit** (the core value — a trusted human shows up).
2. **Daily update to the family** ("Amma had a good day" + a photo — this is the emotional hook).
3. **Medicine reminders** (simple, high-frequency value; you can even do this by a daily WhatsApp/call at first).
4. **Reach-help / SOS** (a phone number *you* answer 24×7 at launch + "call 108" guidance).
5. **A simple check-in** (did the elder eat / take meds / feel okay today).

**Remove/hide initially:** AI companion, video doctor consults, subscription tiers, in-app payments automation, wellness graphs, marketplace of many caregivers.
**Postpone:** everything that isn't one of the 5 above. If a feature doesn't build trust or safety in month 1, it waits.

## Section 3 — Caregiver recruitment (you know no one — here's how)

**Where to find them (Vizag, free):**
- **Nursing colleges / GNM-ANM institutes** (KGH nursing school, Apollo/Care nursing colleges) — placement cells have graduates needing work.
- **GDA (General Duty Assistant) training centres** (NSDC/Healthcare Sector Skill Council partners).
- **Retired nurses** (ask at temples, RWA groups, church notice boards — the most trusted profile).
- **Existing home-nursing agencies** (Portea/Care24-type + local ones) — ask if their caregivers want extra jobs.
- **Facebook/WhatsApp local job groups**, apna.co, WorkIndia, Quikr Jobs (free postings).
- **Word of mouth** — one good caregiver refers three.

**How to contact + convince:** be honest and human. "I'm a student building a service that gets caregivers *respectful, well-paid, regular* work with elders, plus training and an ID card. I pay on time, weekly. Would you like steady jobs?" Caregivers care about **pay reliability + respect + safety** — promise and deliver those.

**Onboarding workflow (free):**
Google Form application → phone screen → document collection → reference calls → in-person/video interview → background check → training → ID card (Canva) → first supervised job.

**Interview questions (score each 1–5):**
1. Tell me about an elder you cared for. What did a typical day look like?
2. An elder refuses to take medicine or eat — what do you do?
3. An elder falls in front of you — walk me through your exact steps.
4. How do you keep an elder's dignity when helping with bathing/toilet?
5. What languages do you speak? Comfortable with Telugu/Hindi elders?
6. Why do you want this work? What does "good care" mean to you?
7. Are you comfortable being tracked during visits and rated by families? (Reliability test.)
8. Any situation you found hard with a family — how did you handle it?

**Verification checklist (do NOT skip):**
- [ ] Govt photo ID (Aadhaar via DigiLocker — don't store the raw number) + a 2nd ID
- [ ] Address proof
- [ ] Skill/nursing certificate (if clinical)
- [ ] 2 contactable references (call them)
- [ ] Recent photo (for ID card + family)
- [ ] **Police character certificate / a paid background check** for anyone doing live-in/overnight
- [ ] Health-fitness self-declaration
- [ ] Signed caregiver agreement + code of conduct

**Training checklist (½–1 day, free, you deliver it):**
- [ ] SETU code of conduct + elder dignity + consent
- [ ] Hygiene + safety basics
- [ ] What to do in an emergency (call 108 first, then family, then you)
- [ ] How you'll coordinate (WhatsApp check-in, photo update, mark visit done)
- [ ] Privacy — never share family info
- [ ] Boundaries — no money handling, no medical decisions

**Pay:** local market — e.g. ₹300–500/visit or ₹800–1,200/day; you take ~15% at first (keep it low to win supply). **Pay weekly, on time, every time.**

## Section 4 — First 10 users (free)

**Rank by warmth of relationship (start warmest):**
1. **Your own family + relatives** with ageing parents/grandparents — your first 1–2 users. They'll forgive rough edges and give honest feedback.
2. **Friends' families / your parents' friends** — the NRI-child angle: friends abroad whose parents are in Vizag.
3. **Your apartment/gated community** (and nearby ones) — RWA WhatsApp groups, notice boards, a stall at the community gate on a weekend.
4. **Senior-citizen associations** (Vizag has active ones) + morning-walk groups at Beach Road / parks.
5. **Temples, churches, community halls** — put up a simple flyer, talk to people after services.
6. **One friendly local doctor / small clinic** — ask to refer patients who need home care.
7. **Local Facebook/WhatsApp community groups** (Vizag localities).

**The ask (simple, honest):** "I'm a student from Vizag building a trusted, verified home elder-care service. I'm taking my first 5 families **free/at-cost for a month** to prove it works. Do you know anyone whose parents need help — meals, medicines, company, or emergency support?"

**Free/discounted first month = your best growth tool.** You're buying feedback + testimonials, not revenue, in month 1.

## Section 5 — Trust building (without money)

**"Why should I trust SETU with my mother?"**

- **30-second pitch:** "I'm a Vizag student who saw families struggle to care for elderly parents from far away. SETU sends a *verified, police-checked, trained* caregiver, sends you a daily update with a photo, and gives one-tap emergency help. You always know your mother is safe — even from another city."
- **1-minute pitch:** *(30-sec) +* "Every caregiver is background-verified, carries a SETU ID card, and is rated by families like you. You see who's coming, get a daily 'she had a good day' update, and can reach help any time — we guide you to 108 and alert you instantly. I personally oversee every family right now, so if anything's wrong, you call *me*. I'm starting with just 5 families so each gets my full attention."
- **5-minute pitch:** *(1-min) +* the problem (India's ageing + distant/NRI children + no trusted option), how verification works step by step, the caregiver's training, what a typical day/update looks like, the emergency flow (108-first), pricing transparency (no hidden fees), your personal accountability, and one real story from your first family. End with: "I'm not asking you to trust a company. I'm asking you to trust *me*, and I'll earn it one day at a time."

**Trust without funding:**
- **Your own face + name + phone number.** As founder, *you* are the trust. Be reachable.
- **Do the first visits yourself or accompany the caregiver.** Nothing builds trust like the founder showing up.
- **Show, don't tell:** the caregiver's verified ID card, their certificate, references, and a signed agreement.
- **Transparency:** honest pricing, a simple written consent + policy, and "here's exactly what we do and don't do."
- **Testimonials:** after week 1, ask your first family for 2 lines + a photo. Social proof is free and priceless.
- **Certifications to show:** caregiver's skill/nursing cert, your (basic) business registration, and — as soon as you can afford it — **insurance**. Until then, be honest that you're early and personally accountable.

## Section 6 — Operations with 5 users (you are everything)

**Booking:** a Google Sheet + WhatsApp. Family messages you → you schedule the caregiver → confirm in the group.
**Emergencies:** *your* phone is the SOS line, 24×7, at launch. Protocol: caller → tell them call 108 immediately → you call the caregiver + family → you go/coordinate → write it down. (This is exhausting but it's only 5 families; it's how you learn what to automate.)
**Caregivers:** daily morning WhatsApp check ("all visits confirmed?"), evening check ("all done? any issues?"), weekly pay.
**Support:** you answer every message within minutes. This *is* your product right now.
**Complaints:** listen, apologise, fix fast, follow up. A well-handled complaint creates a loyal customer.

**Your workflows:**
- **Daily:** morning — confirm today's visits + caregivers. Midday — check any issues. Evening — collect updates/photos, send families their daily note, note problems. Night — reply to messages.
- **Weekly:** pay caregivers. Call each of the 5 families for honest feedback. Review what broke. Recruit 1–2 new caregivers. Post 1 update on social.
- **Monthly:** tally the metrics (Section 11). Decide: is this working? What to fix? Should I take on 5 more families?

## Section 7 — Money tiers (what each buys)

- **₹0:** WhatsApp + Sheets + Forms + Canva + your time + free tiers. Run the concierge MVP with 3–5 families. Invest in: *nothing but your effort.* Avoid: everything paid.
- **₹5,000:** printed **ID cards + a few uniforms/aprons + flyers**, one paid background check for a live-in caregiver, transport for your own visits. Invest in *trust artifacts*. Avoid: ads.
- **₹10,000:** the above + **basic caregiver insurance/accident cover** (small policy) + a domain + a simple logo. Invest in *credibility*. Avoid: paid marketing still.
- **₹25,000:** + **sole-proprietor/business registration + GST** if needed, a lawyer to review your (template) agreements once, more background checks, a small referral incentive budget. Invest in *legitimacy + a few more caregivers*.
- **₹50,000:** + **professional-indemnity + public-liability insurance (starter)**, a small local marketing test (community events, printed material), 10–15 verified caregivers. Invest in *scaling supply + protection*.
- **₹1,00,000:** + one part-time **ops helper**, proper insurance, a first small paid-acquisition test aimed at NRIs, and buffer. Invest in *buying back your time* so you can grow past what one person can run.

**Rule:** spend on **trust and supply**, never on vanity (fancy office, big ads, over-built tech). Every rupee should either make a family trust you more or get a caregiver in front of a family.

## Section 8 — Partnerships in Vizag (how to pitch)

*(Approach only after you've served ~10 families and have a story + testimonials.)*
- **Hospitals** (KGH, Apollo, Care, Seven Hills, Medicover): pitch to the **discharge/patient-relations desk** — "I provide verified post-discharge home care; refer patients who need help at home; I'll keep them safe and update the family." Offer reliability, not money, first.
- **Pharmacies** (local + Apollo/MedPlus): "Refer customers who need medicine management/home delivery help; I'll send families to you for refills." Mutual referral.
- **Diagnostic centres** (Vijaya, Lucid, Medall): home sample collection referrals both ways.
- **Senior-citizen associations, Lions/Rotary clubs, HelpAge India (Vizag)**: offer a **free awareness talk** on elder safety; collect interested families.
- **NGOs / old-age homes**: partner for outreach + credibility; volunteer first, ask later.

**Pitch principle:** lead with *"how I help your patients/members,"* not *"what I need from you."* Bring a one-page flyer + your testimonials.

## Section 9 — Marketing with ₹0

- **Word of mouth (your #1 channel):** deliver such good care to 5 families that they *tell others.* Ask directly: "Do you know one other family who could use this?"
- **WhatsApp:** community groups, RWA groups, family groups. A simple, honest message + a flyer image.
- **Instagram + LinkedIn:** document the journey — "Day 12 of building SETU: today Amma's caregiver…" Founder storytelling attracts users, caregivers, and (later) investors for free. Post 3×/week.
- **College network:** your campus, alumni, friends with parents in Vizag; a talk at an entrepreneurship cell; friends abroad (NRIs) whose parents are here.
- **Local communities:** morning-walk groups, temple notice boards, apartment stalls on weekends.
- **Referral strategy:** give a free/discounted visit to any family that refers another. Give caregivers a bonus for referring good caregivers. Referrals compound trust *and* growth.

**Growth strategy:** density, not spread. Own **one or two localities** in Vizag completely before going wider. 10 happy families in one apartment complex beat 10 scattered across the city.

## Section 10 — Legal (what you truly need vs later)

**Needed before you touch a real family (mostly free templates):**
- **Consent form** (elder/family consents to care + data) — a simple signed page.
- **Caregiver agreement + code of conduct** (contractor, confidentiality, conduct).
- **Basic Terms + Privacy note** (you can start with plain-language versions; you already have DPDP-aware drafts).
- **Medical + emergency disclaimers** ("we coordinate care; call 108 in emergencies; we don't provide medical treatment").
- **A refund/cancellation note.**

**Can wait until a little money/traction:**
- Formal company registration (start as **sole proprietor**), GST (only when turnover requires), full lawyer review, formal insurance (get it as soon as you possibly can — it's the highest-priority paid item), trademark.

**Non-negotiable even at ₹0:** honesty about what you are (a student-run early service), 108-first emergency guidance, and never over-promising safety you can't guarantee.

## Section 11 — 30-day real-world test (5 families, 10 caregivers, 1 city)

**Metrics to collect (in your Sheet):**
- Visits scheduled vs completed (reliability %)
- Caregiver on-time % and no-show count
- Family NPS ("would you recommend?" 0–10) + would they **pay**, and how much
- Retention: do families ask for week 2, 3, 4?
- Referrals generated
- Incidents (and how you handled them)
- Your hours spent per family (tells you what to automate)

**Feedback to collect (call each family weekly):**
- What did we do well? What worried you?
- Was the caregiver trustworthy/kind/skilled?
- Would you pay for this? How much feels fair?
- What one thing would make this a "must-have"?

**Problems to watch for:**
- Caregivers no-showing / unreliable (supply is your #1 risk)
- Families loving it but **unwilling to pay** (the killer signal — investigate hard)
- You burning out (unsustainable ops)
- Any safety scare (your response defines you)

**The verdict after 30 days:** if families **retain + pay + refer**, you have a business — scale carefully. If they love it but won't pay, fix the value/pricing before spending. If they don't care, you've learned it cheaply — pivot.

## Section 12 — Roadmap

- **Month 1:** concierge MVP, 3–5 families (free/at-cost), 3–5 caregivers, deliver great care, collect metrics + testimonials. *Build nothing new; validate.*
- **Month 2:** charge money (even small), 10 families, 5–8 caregivers, tighten your SOPs, start the app for the families who want it. First paid retention.
- **Month 3:** 20–30 families in 1–2 localities, ~10 caregivers, first insurance, first partnership talk, referral engine on. Prove it's repeatable + break-even-ish per family.
- **Month 6:** 50–100 families, refined playbook, first part-time help, decide subscription pricing, small NRI marketing test. This is when a repeatable, near-profitable city playbook should exist.
- **Month 12:** 200–500 families, profitable or near it in Vizag, real insurance + registration, a small team, a clear metrics story → **now raise seed funding** (you'll raise on *proof*, not a pitch).
- **Year 2:** city #2 + #3 using the playbook, managed-care + NRI premium, hospital/insurer partnerships.
- **Year 3:** multi-city, B2B2C at scale, the beginnings of the lifetime elder-care ecosystem.

**Hire when:** you're turning away families because you personally can't handle more (usually ~30–50 families). **Raise when:** you have proof — retention, willingness-to-pay, a repeatable city playbook. Not before.

## Section 13 — What NOT to build (brutally honest)

- **AI companion, video consults, wellness graphs, subscription tiers, in-app maps, multi-language, devices** — all *later*. None of these get your first family to trust you.
- **A big admin dashboard / automation** — you ARE the automation at 5 families. Don't build tools for a scale you don't have.
- **A polished brand / expensive design** — a Canva logo is fine. Trust comes from care, not aesthetics.
- **Company registration + GST on Day 1** — sole proprietor is fine until you have revenue.
- **Paid ads** — you have no product-market fit yet; ads just burn money faster.
- **Perfecting the app** — the app is *not* your bottleneck; *proof of paying demand* is. Every hour polishing pixels is an hour not talking to a family.

**The trap:** as a builder, you'll want to *build*. Resist. **Your job now is to sell and serve, not to build.**

## Section 14 — Founder advice (you, sitting across from me, launching tomorrow)

- **What you're overthinking:** the app, the tech, the "perfect" launch, features, scale, competitors. None of it matters until 5 families pay and stay.
- **What you're missing:** *talking to families.* Go have 20 real conversations with families of elderly parents this week. That's worth more than 3 months of building. Also: **insurance** is the one paid thing to prioritise the moment you have any money.
- **What to focus on:** (1) find 3 families with a real, painful need, (2) find 2 caregivers you'd trust with your own grandmother, (3) match them and deliver an *unforgettable* week, (4) ask "would you pay?" and listen.
- **Mistakes to avoid:** building more instead of selling; scaling before it's repeatable; hiding behind the app instead of showing your face; skipping verification to move fast (one bad caregiver ends you); over-promising safety; spending money you don't have.
- **What I'd do as founder:** I'd forget I'm technical. I'd spend 30 days as a *caregiver-and-salesperson*, not an engineer. I'd personally care for 3 families, learn every pain, and let *reality* — not my assumptions — tell me what to build next. I'd keep costs at ₹0 until families were literally handing me money and asking for more.
